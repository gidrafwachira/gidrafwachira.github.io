Building community early warning capacity in Uganda

Competencies: partnerships, systems, learning and scaling

Executive summary

In June 2026, as head of NetHope's Climate Intersections programme, I led this project with ITU, GSMA, UNDRR, Uganda's Office of the Prime Minister and the Ministry of ICT to train district disaster management leaders in community early warning systems (CEWS). The three-day course in Entebbe covered 10 disaster-prone districts: Amuru, Bududa, Butaleja, Kampala, Kamuli, Manafwa, Moroto, Ntungamo, Pakwach and Sironko.

Challenge

Uganda has national hazard data and a monthly early warning bulletin, yet warnings often stall at the district office. Floods, droughts, landslides and lightning hit communities that are remote, poorly connected and last to hear. Roles are unclear, funding is thin and local committees have had little training.

Approach

  • Partnered with the government, which chose districts by hazard exposure, and commissioned an expert facilitator to deliver the training
  • Taught through group work, scenario simulations, peer learning and case studies instead of lectures
  • Mapped how warnings actually flow in each district and where they break down
  • Added a district action plan session, with activities, owners and timelines, to build ownership
  • Measured learning with pre- and post-tests and a participant survey

Results

  • 37 district and sub-county committee members trained, with average test scores up from 77.6% to 97.3% (+19.7 points)
  • 36 of 37 improved, and 19 scored full marks after starting with none
  • No participant rated the programme fair or poor: 57% rated it excellent and 43% good
  • 10 district action plans plus a national set of recommendations on coordination, mobile alerts, budgets and inclusion of women, youth and people with disabilities

Takeaway

Knowledge is quick to build, but lasting change needs funding and follow-up. Only 58% of participants felt fully confident to design a CEWS, so the next step is quarterly action-plan reviews and rolling the training down to parish and village level.
